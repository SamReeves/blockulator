❓ How Gaussian Futures Work

The Hot Potato That Pays You:

A Gaussian Future is a unique on-chain contract that distributes its value over time following a bell curve (normal distribution). Think of it as a hot potato game where passing the potato pays you, not the person catching it!

📈 The Distribution

🎯 How to Play

  1. Deploy: Create a future with ETH and set its lifetime (e.g., 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week). The contract starts with 100% of the value you deposit.
  2. Wait for the Sweet Spot: The contract follows a Gaussian (bell curve) distribution centered at the midpoint. The most value is released when transferring around the middle of the lifetime.
  3. Transfer to Claim: When you transfer ownership to another address, the contract calculates how much time has passed and pays you based on the probability distribution. The earlier or later you transfer, the less you receive. Transfer near the midpoint for maximum payout!
  4. New Owner's Turn: The new owner now controls the contract and can transfer it again to claim their portion of the remaining value.
  5. Expiration: When the lifetime ends, the final owner can claim any remaining ETH in the contract.

🧮 The Math Behind It

The contract uses the Lin 1990 approximation of the Gaussian distribution (normal curve). It calculates your payout based on the "area under the curve" between transfers:

  • Mean (μ): Lifetime ÷ 2 (the center point)
  • Standard Deviation (σ): Lifetime ÷ √12 (controls the curve's spread)
  • Your Payout: Initial Value × (CDF at your transfer time - CDF at previous transfer time)

💡 Strategy Tips

  • ✅ Transfer near the midpoint for maximum value extraction
  • ✅ The contract is fully deterministic - you can calculate expected payouts before transferring
  • ✅ Early transfers and late transfers both receive minimal payouts
  • ⚠️ Each transfer permanently reduces the remaining contract balance
  • ⚠️ Gas costs matter - make sure payouts exceed transaction fees!
📝 Technical Note: All calculations happen on-chain using lookup tables for e^x with 10-decimal precision. No oracles, no external dependencies - pure deterministic smart contract math.
